## What the chain costs today (2026-09-19, local, idle box)

`52bb329` gave two reasons for dropping it: not measurably worse at 109
seeds, and "92% of the traversal this function can do". The first is what
the bisect challenges. The second is measured here, because `access` was
rewritten after the deletion (route counting, `access_decay`) and the old
cost claim was about code that no longer exists.

| measurement | chain off | chain on | |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `access` over all 84 countries | 0.034 ms | 0.068 ms | **2.0x** |
| `rank_actions` over 120 corpus positions | 28.5 ms mean | 29.8 ms mean | **1.05x** |
| a whole self-play game (seeds 4000, 4002) | 28.4 s, 25.4 s | 55.1 s, 28.9 s | confounded |

**The 2x is real and almost irrelevant.** `access` doubles, and `access` is
about 5% of a ranking, so a decision costs 5% more on the same position --
and that 5% already includes the wider dependents radius the chain forces.
The top action changes on 2 of 120 positions, so the term is wired in and
the timing is of the thing we mean (a 0 there would be bug shape 8).

**Whole-game wall clock is not the measurement.** Seed 4001 ran 5.3 s with
the chain off and 68.9 s with it on -- because the off game ended on turn 5
and the on game went to turn 10. Different games, not a slower bot. Any
per-game timing of a term that changes play measures the games, which is
why the corpus positions are the honest comparison.

## If it needs to be cheaper anyway

Three savings, in the order they are worth taking. The first two are
verified to produce identical values on every country:

1. **Precompute the candidate pairs.** `(n, m)` with `m` a battleground two
   hops out through `n` and not adjacent to `i` is pure terrain, so it is an
   `lru_cache`d tuple per country -- mean 1.5 pairs, max 6, against a double
   loop with a set membership test today.
2. **`near` instead of the inner scan.** `any(inf_s[k] > 0 for k in
   neighbors[m])` is exactly `pos.near[s][m] > 0`, which the snapshot
   already maintains incrementally. O(1) instead of a walk.
   Together these take the chain's own cost down about 20% (0.068 -> 0.061
   ms over 84 countries).
3. **The radius is the bigger cost, and it can be exact.** The chain forces
   `VALUE_RADIUS` 2 -> 3, which is 11.8 countries re-valued per trial
   placement against 7.4. But a radius-3 ball is wider than what the terms
   read: the third hop enters only through `near[m]` for battleground chain
   targets. The exact read set, computed statically from the same geometry,
   averages **9.7** -- 40% of the radius penalty back, and it is a tighter
   contract than a ball, not a looser one.

None of this is worth building until an arm says the term is worth keeping.
If one does, item 3 is the one to build, and it wants the same negative
control as `value_radius`: scan every country, assert nothing outside the
claimed set moved.
