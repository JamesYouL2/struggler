## The hang, and what it cost -- RESOLVED 06:50

A benchmark run reached 511 of 512 games and then sat on the last one --
**seed 7500, USSR seat, weights `{access: 0.0}`** -- for **four hours** at
97% CPU, blocking the rest of `overnight.sh` and all of `overnight2.sh`
behind it. Roughly half the night. The maintainer killed both queues.

**The ablation caused its own hang.** Zeroing `access` removes a whole
discriminating family, so positions that differed only in that term tie
*exactly*, and whatever breaks ties explores far more. That is a result
worth keeping: `access` earns its place on search efficiency even if it
turns out not to on strength.

**Nothing caught it, for three separate reasons, all now fixed:**

1. `benchmark.py` had no per-game timeout. It now takes `--stall-timeout`
   (default 1200s), which measures the gap between *finishes across all
   workers* rather than the length of any one game -- a slow game is fine
   as long as something is completing.
2. The report was only written after the dispatch loop, so killing the run
   by hand lost all 511 finished games. The stall path now terminates the
   pool and reports what completed. (An empty-games guard came with it:
   `summarize([])` raised from `statistics.fmean` rather than saying what
   happened.)
3. Early stopping could never have engaged. The weight A/B path passed
   neither `--decide` **nor** `--held-seeds`, and `_decided` is gated on a
   held sample existing -- so the flag alone would have done nothing. Both
   queue scripts now split their range in half and pass both.

**The ablation is now run at `access: 0.01`, not `0.0`** -- the
maintainer's instruction, and the better experiment as well as the safer
one. Zero conflates "does this term's magnitude matter" with "does this
term discriminate at all". At 0.01 the ordering survives while ~99% of the
magnitude is gone, isolating the first question.

## What is settled

- **`vp_swing` is close to inert.** Two independent lines: the fitted
  win-probability slope is flat from T3 (spread 7.45 -> 6.86, a ratio of
  1.09), and 4x measured **0.491 +/-0.026** over 256 seeds. 1.0 is queued
  and unrun. The shipped value is 2.0.
- **The VP curve shipped and passed.** Gate at `91f26d1`: pooled
  **0.513 +/-0.033** over 78 seeds, ACCEPTED.
- **Board value collapses early, and that is where the effect is.** An
  empty stability-2 Battleground, 349 captured positions:
  **T1 5.87 VP, T2 3.20, T3 2.58**. Measuring the best Battleground of any
  occupancy, which survives past T3: 5.87, 4.48, 5.16, then **2.11 at T4**,
  where 46 Mid War cards enter the deck. The maintainer's reading -- Ops
  first on turn 1, value VP aggressively after -- is supported, and the
  cliff is at T4.
- **The rankings are knife-edge.** Removing one phantom card from a
  thirty-card unseen pool reordered **22.7%** of corpus rankings. A change
  that reordered 4% produced no measurable strength difference. Ranking
  churn and strength are close to unrelated here; do not read a parity
  delta as a strength signal.

## Open, in the order I would take them

1. **`scoring_schedule` prices future scorings as certain.** It caps at the
   horizon, but anything inside the horizon is priced at full weight, while
   `FINAL_SCORING_ODDS` says a turn-1 game reaches final scoring 22% of the
   time. `_scoring_weight_uncached` applies survival odds to exactly one
   term and treats every other as certain, with the machinery imported one
   line away. Probably worth more than either queued experiment.
2. **The board-side terms.** `scoring_final` is the only bucket that rises
   monotonically across the game (0.222 -> 1.0), so it is the only lever
   that can make board value rise late. Queued in `overnight2.sh`.
3. **The deck tracker.** The uniform posterior is in. The sharp version
   needs one fact carried across the reshuffle -- which cards were in the
   pile when it emptied -- from which about **half the opponent's turn-3
   hand is known with certainty**, not probability. It is *memory*, and
   shape 1 in `bug-shapes.md` (state keyed on less than it reads) has
   recurred seven times, so it wants its own gate and invariant test.
4. **The maintainer's judgement calls**: delete `access` if the ablation
   ever accepts; formally drop 4x; pick a replacement for the `margin`
   ablation (dropped -- two of its four weights are gate-sourced, so
   zeroing it is not the untested-constants experiment).

## Conventions that matter here

- **The gate's exit status is the verdict.** "ACCEPTED" means *not
  measurably worse*, never "better". Piping it into `tail` hands you
  tail's status.
- **One gate at a time** -- now enforced with a lock, because two at the
  same HEAD delete each other's baseline (`snapshot` opens with `rm -rf`).
  `GATE_WAIT=1` queues instead of refusing.
- **Never ask `pgrep` whether something is running.** Four recurrences,
  shape 9. Use `scripts/gate_running.py`.
- **Do not edit a running bash script.** Bash reads it incrementally.
- **Sample size from the effect expected.** At 256 seeds the half-width is
  +/-0.026, measured -- not the +/-0.020 the mirror-gate constant predicts.
  Some changes are unresolvable at any affordable size: the Space Race
  fix touches 1.6% of plays.
- `logs/` is gitignored. Commit generated notes, not reports.
